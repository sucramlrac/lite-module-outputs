Part 1

Personal Cybersecurity Checkup

In the middle of the night, sometimes I ask myself: am I really safe and protected online? I have had many accounts over the years for various purposes like gaming, school, and personal use. Aligned with my interests, I also had the relentless pursuit of accessing dangerous websites on my old computer. Therefore, I do not have a history of good cyber hygiene and security.

I had made accounts to access compromised websites. I found out about online leakages, and unfortunately one of my accounts was leaked. I checked using the online tool HaveIBeenPwned if my email address is part of a data breach. As of now it is part of five data breaches. Once I found out, I changed my password immediately. I made other accounts and learned from the situation. In fact, I barely access the compromised account.

Now, I have a laptop and a phone. In both devices, I enable features that make them more secure. I have good habits such as not connecting to unsecure networks, avoiding suspicious files, and installing content blockers. I also segregate my activity to different accounts, and try my best to avoid cookies. Thus, I think my cyber habits contribute to my cybersecurity.

If one of my accounts is compromised, the most damaging is my personal account which contains my personal data and sensitive personal data. Though I enabled MFA and security features such as recovery emails, it will still be anxiety-inducing as it is the account of my most active online social activities. Being compromised, there is no telling what attackers may do. Therefore, I must protect it as it will hinder my life if it is at risk.

In conclusion, although I do not have a history of good cyber hygiene, I try to improve moving forward. Compromised accounts must be cleaned and stronger habits must be formed so that previous compromising habits are overwritten. My personal account must be protected with extra layers of protection. That way, I can be more secure and at ease.

Part 2

Phishing Awareness Investigation

For my Phishing Analysis Portfolio, I will focus on SMS phishing which is prominent in my area.

  1. The "Lottery Win" Scenario

    These kinds of messages hook the recipient with unclaimed rewards, creating a sense of excitement. They say you won a big amount of money, unclaimed mobile data rewards, and raffle prizes like the latest smartphones. One common feature is a link attached to the message, which is definitely a way for the attacker to exploit the user. Be wary of SMS which gives a sense of excitement and external links.

  2. The "Contact Me ASAP" Scenario

    This type of message imitates an urgent request from another person which you may or may not know about. They usually have emails attached where the attacker instructs to contact you with. The question to ask here is why do we need to contact each other in another platform if SMS is enough? Take note of important contacts and who you give your contact number to.

  3. The "Channel Spoofing" Scenario

    These SMS imitate the channel name of well-known banks and official channels which may catch users off-guard. Never click links in SMS, because they might steal personal information as soon as you click them. Remember that official SMS channels do not put external links in their SMS.

Part 3

Filter Bubble and Echo Chamber Reflection

I am prone to the filter bubble and the echo chamber of opinions I gather from social media. Most of my online sources, like Facebook, Instagram, and social media news outlets amplify my preexisting beliefs, especially in religion, politics, and socioeconomic opinions.

There are times my social media connections share interesting posts that I agree with, and I see it in my timeline. I assume it is because of the algorithm which flags post that are catered towards me, and it also depends on how much interaction I have with certain connections.

Regarding different opinions, I barely see them. Unless I search for them specifically and utter shock about the contrasting opinions I have with other people. Certain groups I do not interact with and disagree with, as well as posts I have no particular interest in, do not appear in my feed. I believe it is the work of the algorithm. There are discovery channels in these social media platforms, but I barely keep up with new trends.

Say I liked a post about animals like otters and cats, then I am certain more of them will appear soon in my feed. Interacting with certain tech pages will give me more technology centered advertisements. Lastly, my interaction with another user will most likely be used as a flag to have more frequent updates from them.

Therefore, my opinions and beliefs are enforced by the social media algorithm. Although I think it is possible to control your personalized feed by, let's say, liking more dog posts than cat posts, then in the future I will surely get more canine content.

Part 5

Final Reflection

What surprised me the most in this activity is how much I get profiled and how much personalized content I'm getting. I am more prone to doom scrolling and mindless consumption of social media. I have just realized that what I consume more frequently shapes my beliefs, and in turn shares my feed, and it is a cycle of consumption. My overreliance to major services and platforms risks my privacy and identity. Therefore, I will be more mindful in how I share the data and how I consume my feed so I can avoid psychological side effects of using social media.
