Part 1

My Life in the Information Society

My whole life revolves around using information communication technologies (ICTs), not just as a tool; but as a part of my routine, communication, relationships, education, and career.

My day starts with an alarm, using my smartphone which is connected to a time server from the internet. Reading missed messages while asleep and sending good morning messages to loved ones.

During university course classes, difficult weather conditions and government-mandated suspensions produces learning loss. But fret not – the student learning management system allows me to keep up with my academics, paired with the power of internet, search engines, and artificial intelligence to aid my self-learning.

As a computer science student, access to technology and online resources is a must. In programming and development, the trend is now about cloud infrastructure and machine learning, which every computer science and information technology students will inevitably learn. Looking for internships and possible job opportunities involves lots of social media and networking apps such as LinkedIn and email services. Even my potential work setting could be hybrid or totally work from home.

Hearing stories from my parents, I've reached a summary of the difference between my generation and theirs: appreciation of life. Technology made it way easier to achieve things and meet my basic needs. Comparably, my parents found ways around the challenges of life without using today's intelligent technologies, while I am lost without them and on my own devices (you can interpret this phrase in any way).

Since it is much easier to achieve things, appreciation of life becomes shallower. Appreciation for life outside ICTs is overdue. Therefore, I realized I would like to be more in touch with real life than with my ICT life. Isolation due to digital addiction is a sad thing, and there are finer things in life worth my attention other than using ICTs.

Part 2

My Personal Technology Timeline

  1. First Exposure to computers – Age 3, Year 2009, where I played computer games in the genre of real time strategy (RTS) and Madagascar PC Video Game. Also played Warcraft and Frozen Throne (RTS). While using the computer, a digital encyclopedia was installed for me to learn in: I vaguely remember an article about the meteor impact on earth which wiped out the dinosaurs and a 3D exploration of a castle. Another is a 3D interactive software about animals where I learned about animal sounds (likely Zoo Tycoon 2). I played PS2 games with my family such as fighting games and platformer games. Probably, this is the start of why I am interested in video games, 3D, and sciences.
  2. First Internet experience – Facebook 2013. My father made us Facebook accounts, and we post albums in our timeline. This is important, because I felt never had opportunities to leave the house and go to friends, and Facebook made me connect with classmates and my introduction to social media and the internet, mostly Google.
  3. First Smartphone – Samsung Galaxy Prime 2017 at Grade 6. I've had a tablet with games installed such as Minecraft and Mobile Legends, but I received my first phone as a hand me down from my father. This made it easier to contact my family, but from here on out I drifted away from communicating with my family and preferred to message friends instead. Now, however, I realize that it was not the right thing because I had isolated myself.
  4. First Cloud-Based Application – Skype 2010 Long Distance Video Call (UAE to Philippines). My mother was an OFW, and we used broadband internet to connect with her. Most of my childhood, my mother was not present, and my father was working almost every day and sometimes stays at his job. Most of my childhood was with my lola, titos, and brothers. Through long distance phone calls and Skype, we were able to stay in touch.
  5. Most important technology you use today – social media (particularly Facebook and YouTube), which has been a great communication tool. It was also a great entertainment provider, where I played games such as Dragon City, Candy Crush, and many more. I learned more about global affairs and other culture. I watched video game creators like PewDiePie, and learned through video tutorials. In 2017, I played video games and joined online communities in the Discord platform, and it really changed the social aspect of my life.
Part 3

My Digital Citizenship Identity

Aspect Description
Digital Identity

I present myself in due proper netiquette online.

My online identity is fragmented; I have many different accounts and personas for various purposes.
Digital Footprint

Personal information such as my name, date of birth, address, and other identifying information lurks everywhere, under different email addresses.

It makes me connected to any other person, however I am at risk for identity theft, tracking, and hijacking.
Responsible Technology Use

Before sharing information, I like to cross-examine different sources to verify facts.

I protect my privacy and security by rejecting cookies as much as possible, using the privacy and security measures in apps, and by not giving away personal information easily.
Digital Well-being

I spend around 12hrs online daily.

Technology and being online daily affects my physical health as it drains my sleep. My social and emotional health as it isolates me. And my mental health as I slowly get more addicted.

Personal Action Plan

My Specific, Measurable, Attainable, Relevant, and Timebound (SMART) goals.

  1. I will be mindful of how I message and communicate online by using proper grammar and include more context with my text messages for the next 30 days so I can be more responsible in using information and communication technologies.
  2. I will limit my watching of shows to two episodes per day for the next 30 days so I can improve my well-being and focus.
  3. I will limit my recreational social media use to 1 hour for the next 30 days so I can improve my well-being and focus.
